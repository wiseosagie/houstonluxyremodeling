## Wave 2 — Keyword Planner Analysis

**Source:** `Keyword Stats 2026-09-20 at 21_50_15.csv`, Google Keyword Planner export, UTF-16
encoded, 1,383 data rows, date range Sept 2025–Aug 2026. Parsed programmatically (not by hand) to
avoid transcription error.

**Critical caveat:** this export contains **zero** Houston or neighborhood-specific keywords. It
is a national/regional bathroom-and-kitchen-remodeling seed export. It is used here to understand
*relative* demand between broad categories (bathroom vs. kitchen vs. whole-home vs. contractor
intent) and CPC/commercial-value signal — not as a source of Houston-specific search volume, which
does not exist in Google Keyword Planner at usable granularity for a market this size. No
Houston-specific volume number appears anywhere in this document; where a volume figure is cited,
it is explicitly a national figure used for relative prioritization only.

**Volume floor artifact:** Google Keyword Planner buckets low-traffic keywords at a floor of ~50
average monthly searches rather than reporting exact small numbers. Of the 1,383 rows, **1,174
(85%) sit at exactly this 50/mo floor**, 120 show 0, and 13 show no data at all. Only **76 keywords
(5.5%)** have real, differentiated volume (two at 5,000/mo, seventy-four at 500/mo). Prioritization
below relies on those 76 keywords plus competition/CPC signals; the 50-floor long tail is used only
for qualitative topic coverage, never for volume ranking, since Planner cannot actually
differentiate demand within that tier.

### Strategic Finding: Luxury Is Positioning, Not a Keyword

Every "luxury"-prefixed or "high-end"-prefixed remodeling term in the 1,383-row dataset sits at the
50/mo floor, and the overwhelming majority show **no bid data at all** — meaning advertisers
essentially aren't targeting these exact phrases. Examples: "luxury home remodeling" (comp index
0, no bid), "luxury kitchen remodel" (comp index 5, no bid), "luxury bathroom remodel" (comp index
15, no bid), "high end remodeling" (comp Medium 62, no bid). Compare this to the un-prefixed
versions: "kitchen remodel" (500/mo, bid up to $180.38) and "bathroom remodel" (5,000/mo, bid up to
$43.06). This is hard, quantified confirmation of Section 7's premise: affluent homeowners
searching for a $300K kitchen renovation overwhelmingly type "kitchen remodel Houston," not "luxury
kitchen remodel Houston." Every Wave 2 page targets the broad phrase as primary and uses content,
scope, and imagery — not the word "luxury" in the query — to qualify the audience.

### Excluded Categories (with reasoning)

- **Real-estate browsing** (mansions for sale, luxury homes for sale, luxury real estate agents,
  Sotheby's/Coldwell Banker/Zillow luxury listings): buyer intent, not renovation intent. Someone
  searching "mansions for sale" wants to buy a home, not remodel one. Excluded even where volume
  looks attractive (e.g. 500/mo for several terms).
- **National bath-conversion franchise / brand terms** (Bath Fitter, Re-Bath, Kohler, PowerHome
  Remodeling, DreamStyle, Bath Planet, West Shore Bath/Home, BCI Bath and Shower, plus Lowe's/Home
  Depot/Houzz/IKEA remodel searches): brand-navigational searches for specific national low-cost,
  often one-day acrylic tub-liner or shower-conversion installers. Wrong price tier and wrong
  service model for a $100K+ matching platform; excluded regardless of volume (e.g. "powerhome
  remodeling" 5,000/mo — excluded as a company-name search, not a category search).
- **Small-scope single-room categories** (basement, garage, deck, driveway, staircase, closet,
  laundry room, home office, patio, fireplace, window replacement, countertop-only, cabinet
  refacing, attic): real search terms but well below HLR's $100K+ positioning and outside the five
  priority project categories in the business brief.
- **Tub-to-shower conversion / jacuzzi bath remodel cluster** (e.g. "jacuzzi bath remodel" 500/mo,
  "tub to shower conversion" 50/mo but bid up to $50.48): numerically inflates the "bathroom"
  category total, but represents a fundamentally different, lower-ticket product (acrylic
  liner/conversion installation) than the substantial bathroom renovations HLR facilitates.
  Excluded from the bathroom page's primary targeting even though it shares the word "bathroom."
- **"Small bathroom remodel" cluster** (500/mo, comp High(99), but -90% YoY): the volume-vs-value
  example named directly in the brief. High competition, declining trend, and lower typical
  project value than a full bathroom or primary suite transformation. Not targeted as primary on
  any page; the bathroom page's content explicitly differentiates cosmetic-refresh scope from the
  substantial-renovation scope it's built to attract.

## Cannibalization Notes

- **Pillar vs. Cluster A pages.** `/luxury-remodeling-houston` contains a card grid linking to each
  dedicated category page rather than full category write-ups, avoiding duplicate deep content.
- **Kitchen page primary-target change.** `/luxury-kitchen-remodeling-houston` now leads with
  "Kitchen Remodeling Houston" (title, H1, eyebrow, breadcrumb) instead of "Luxury Kitchen
  Remodeling Houston," per the Wave 2 CSV evidence that the un-prefixed phrase carries 10x the
  volume and far higher CPC. **The URL was not changed** — only on-page title/H1/heading targeting,
  consistent with Section 37's URL-stability guidance. This is a legitimate example of a page
  keeping its URL while its primary keyword target is refined by real data.
- **Bathroom vs. primary suite.** `/luxury-bathroom-remodeling-houston` owns "bathroom
  remodeling/renovation Houston" as a standalone room project (any bathroom, considered on its
  own). `/primary-suite-remodeling-houston` owns the combined bedroom + closet + bathroom suite
  project. Each page now explicitly cross-links to the other for disambiguation ("if your project
  is X, see Y instead") so neither competes for the other's primary intent.
- **Contractor-hiring intent.** Evaluated a dedicated commercial page (`/remodeling-contractors-
  houston`) given the CSV's strong, rising volume for "remodeling contractors"/"remodeling
  contractor" (+900%/+900%). **Decision: deferred, existing guide optimized instead.** Reasoning:
  (1) a page titled around "remodeling contractors Houston" risks implying HLR operates a
  contractor directory or is itself a contractor, which the business positioning explicitly
  prohibits; (2) `/guides/how-to-choose-remodeling-contractor-houston` already owns this intent
  editorially and a new page would cannibalize it; (3) the safer, truthful move is to broaden that
  guide's on-page targeting (done — see Cluster D) and revisit a dedicated commercial page only if
  Search Console shows the guide capturing meaningful contractor-intent impressions without
  converting, which would indicate a genuinely distinct commercial page is needed.
- **Cost cluster.** Each of the four cost guides (`home-remodeling-cost-houston`,
  `whole-home-remodel-cost-houston`, `kitchen-remodel-cost-houston`, `bathroom-remodel-cost-
  houston`) owns a distinct scope and links to the others only where genuinely relevant (e.g. the
  bathroom cost guide points to the whole-home cost guide only for multi-room projects), avoiding
  duplicate generic cost paragraphs.
- **Cross-cutting factual content** (foundation/clay soil, flood plain, permitting, humidity/
  ventilation) appears on multiple pages by design, each from a different angle: pillar page
  (introductory), additions page (structural-load angle), whole-home cost guide (cost-driver
  angle), bathroom page (ventilation/older-plumbing angle, new in Wave 2), neighborhood pages
  (jurisdiction-specific permitting). No two pages repeat the same paragraph.

## Research Method Note

Wave 1 keyword and SERP research was done via live web search (qualitative SERP composition,
People Also Ask patterns, competitor page structure) — no keyword-volume tool available. Wave 2
re-validated priorities against an actual Google Keyword Planner export (1,383 rows, parsed
programmatically). The two sources cover different gaps: Wave 1 covers Houston/neighborhood/
design-build/permitting intent (none of which appears in the Wave 2 CSV); Wave 2 covers national
relative-demand and CPC signal for bathroom/kitchen/whole-home/contractor categories (which Wave 1
could only assess qualitatively). Re-validate both against actual Google Search Console
impression/click data once these pages have been indexed for 4–8 weeks — that is the only source
that will ever show true Houston-specific demand.
